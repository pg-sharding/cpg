Subject: GIN required-entry heap: selective OR under natural planner choices

Hi,

I completed a separate SQL follow-up for the unchanged required-entry heap
prototype 03dff8bfa5075e5838c7fbffc712d0b79d03f13d. It uses the same fixed-B72
builds and qualified fixture, with B/an exact-ELF twin/Heap. Seven fixed ORs
contain group terms without common, at widths 1/2/7/8/32/128/512, plus an
ID-range control. All scan types are enabled under frozen GUCs; no source,
threshold, costs or statistics were tuned after observing plans.

The actual planner chose GIN bitmap paths through width 128, Seq at 512, and
Index Only for the ID control in every observation. Natural GIN width32
executor Heap/B paired medians were −10.53%/−10.30%, with B/Heap medians
0.847/0.753 ms and 0.8195/0.727 ms. At 128 they were −11.43%/−11.46%, with
11.4115/10.096 ms and 11.359/10.0825 ms. Backend CPU, client elapsed and SQL
Execution Time have separate columns; these are not old AM loop timings.

There are material negatives. Width 8 was −5.50%/+16.31% executor; below-threshold
width 7 was +4.20%/+5.57% (+13.68%/+11.40% backend CPU). At width 1 the cohort 0
exact-twin executor median was +56.13% around a 0.046 ms baseline median.
Even width 512's Seq path showed −3.86%/−3.97% Heap/B, which cannot be attributed
to the required-entry heap execution. The Index Only control was +0.21%/+1.42%.
The cause of unchanged-path variation is unidentified; no control correction
or general adoption/source-causal claim is made.

The fixed matrix retained all 384 raw / 288 retained / 96 warm commands, two fresh
cohorts, all six role orders and all three pairs. Separate untimed forced
Seq/GIN ordered oracles and frozen residue-formula scalar/digest answers
passed. An independent arithmetic audit checked 8208 numbers; full raw plans,
absolute min/median/max/sample SD/MAD and every direct pair are retained.
The final root live check verified all nine owned lifetimes stopped, no owned
PIDs/sockets, all three installed prefixes, 7726 source files, two seeds and
frozen inputs unchanged. No second-host or new portable Linux run is claimed.

The inherited heap idea and shared-entry context were discussed by Heikki
Linnakangas in 2014:
https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/52F373CC.4050800%40vmware.com
This follow-up adds workload evidence, not a new source idea. Earlier
common-superset forced-GIN SQL and actual-AM results remain separate, with
all their negative controls preserved.
