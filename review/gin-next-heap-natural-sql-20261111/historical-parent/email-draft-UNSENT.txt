Subject: [WIP] GIN required-entry copied-key heap: wide-OR AM gains and SQL negatives

Hi,

I implemented a per-key min-heap for at least eight required GIN entries.
Heap keys are copied TID lower bounds. If another key advances a shared
GinScanEntry, the copied bound remains monotone and the stale root is
repaired before returning the minimum. Tiny/fuzzy/exclude-only cases keep
the baseline path, and the consistent/entryRes/additional-entry handling
remains in place. This is independent on fixed B72, not combined with C11
or the bitmap-offset search prototype.

Heikki Linnakangas proposed heap-based GIN stream merging and explicitly
noted the shared-entry problem in 2014:
https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/52F373CC.4050800%40vmware.com
I am not claiming a new heap idea. The concrete contribution here is a
copied-lower-bound lazy repair implementation plus separately checked
shared-entry, valid-stream and actual-AM measurements. Source commit:
03dff8bfa5075e5838c7fbffc712d0b79d03f13d.

All three fresh full builds passed 239 core tests and the posting-list
test, including an assert candidate. Normal and UBSan valid-stream runs
agreed on 2080 cases, covering equal minima, root finishing, stale nonroot
advancement, shared streams and lossy TIDs. Real-AM/SQL qualification used
ordered independent Seq/GIN multisets and frozen scalars. An untimed actual
array_ops scan probe, built with each variant's private headers, observed
NULL extra_data, pointer sharing, unique-versus-summed entry counts and
heap thresholds. Tsquery multiqual alone was not mistaken for deduplication.

The fixed screen used B/exact twin/Heap, two CPU/clone placements and six
retained paired rounds after two warm rounds: 576 raw / 432 retained / 144
warm commands. Actual-AM loop CPU at OR widths 8/32/128/512 improved by
11.98/22.65/28.32/27.12% in cohort 0 and 11.67/22.52/26.50/23.90% in
cohort 1. The width-512 B/Heap CPU medians were 1113.065/810.601 ms and
1108.378/842.687 ms. Startup allocation and complete bitmap consumption
are included in these AM clocks.

There are important negatives. Widths 1/2/7 were 1.1% to 2.0% slower.
SQL sharing8 regressed +1.83% / +2.04%, sharing32 +3.02% / +3.86%, and
absent512 +1.19% / +2.00%. Exact-twin Seq drift was -0.57% / +4.97%.
Thus this is a useful wide-OR AM result, not a general SQL upgrade or a
source-causal claim for small differences. No repeats, source tuning,
trimming, pooling, control normalization or case removal followed.

The packet retains all absolute AM/backend CPU, wall and SQL executor
metrics, min/median/max/mean/sample SD/MAD, every direct pair, raw full
plans, source and setup/reproduction scripts. All six screen clusters
stopped and source/install/module/seed/input hashes remained unchanged.
Original Linux execution is distinguished from offline/static replay.
This finite synthetic serial evidence is not concurrency or general merge
qualification; no second-host or write-operation result is claimed here.
