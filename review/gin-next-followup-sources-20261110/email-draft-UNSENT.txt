Subject: [SOURCE-ONLY DRAFT, UNSENT] GIN decoder and memo regression follow-ups

The completed independent D1/K1 trials retain both target gains and material
negatives. D1 long-delta2 decode CPU regressed about39-41%; K1 dense actual-AM
CPU regressed about14%, while full-read/OR SQL CPU improved about7% on the
shared-load diagnostic. The original packets include all absolute/spread/pair
results and environmental limits; these are not clean-host conclusions.

D2 tests the first continuation bit before the four-byte batch bounds/OR. K2
uses a fixed64 cumulative-miss budget but could lose later OR reuse. K3 uses
64 consecutive misses, resets on a memo hit and then selects the original
direct builtin helper for the rest of the scan key. Exact builtin guards,
ternary results, recheck and ordinary callback fallbacks remain unchanged.

These are independent source-only alternatives, not a series. None has yet
been compiled, qualified or timed in this snapshot; they do not establish a
regression fix. Four fresh B/K1/K3/K3_assert builds, native64-boundary checks,
ordinary SQL and the original23-cell controls precede any new latency claim.
The threshold is fixed before observing results.

Consistent-result caching is direct prior art from Heikki's2014 discussion[1].
No novelty claim is made for caching itself. These concrete cost-control and
short-circuit hypotheses require measurement; nothing has been mailed.
