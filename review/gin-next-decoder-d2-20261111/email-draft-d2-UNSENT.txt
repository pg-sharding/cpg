Subject: GIN posting-list decode: check the leading delta before the four-item batch

I measured a small revision of the earlier D1 four-one-byte GIN posting-list decoder prototype. D2 checks the first delta's continuation bit before checking batch capacity and combining the next three bytes. It preserves D1's four-item batch, allocation policy, scalar fallback and on-disk format. The original PostgreSQL decoder and the prior D1 prototype are the starting point; this is a guard-order experiment, not a new encoding or algorithm claim.

The standalone D2 commit is a85a9242f455b12c93528f3ace33eb69b6cd1008, over fixed B 8a8c475037bfde101da23cc9b0364c9c020eca93. The packet includes its format-patch, standalone diff and the small D1-to-D2 review diff. D1 and D2 are alternatives over the same baseline, not a patch series. Related earlier GIN performance discussion is at https://www.postgresql.org/message-id/52F373CC.4050800%40vmware.com ; that is broader GIN context, not a direct match for this decoder change. No fresh prior-art completeness or novelty claim is made here.

In two fresh matched placements on one x86-64 VM, the actual backend decoder (including allocation, materialization and free) reduces D1's delta2 penalty from +37.86/+38.11% versus B to +4.52/+5.12%. D2/D1 is -24.04/-24.08%. Dense128 retains a -10.71/-10.80% gain against B, although D2 is +3.35/+3.08% slower than D1 on that case. Delta3 and delta4 still regress by +7.40/+6.81% and +6.21/+5.49% against B. Tail2 remains +5.71/+5.86%; other small tails and some batch/growth comparisons are also negative. These observations support a tradeoff, not a universal decoder improvement.

Whole-query SQL is mixed. Skew1000 improves -2.81/-2.46% executor time, from B medians 76.6900/77.6275 ms to D2 74.5960/75.7105 ms for the complete 100-probe command. Dense SQL is +2.25/-0.29%, rare +0.80/+1.67%, forced single +0.87/+0.16%, forced OR +0.30/+0.58%, and prefix +0.02/-0.33%. The ID-only Seq control is +0.58/+1.32%, with identical-ELF twin T/B -0.78/+1.32%. All 18 native and seven SQL cells, backend CPU, client wall, SQL executor/native inner CPU, raw ranges and all six direct role pairs are retained in the packet. Percentages are medians of twelve direct per-round ratios, not ratios of absolute medians. Cohorts are kept separate.

The run used fresh matched GCC -O2 -g -fno-lto full builds, unchanged core239/posting-list tests and actual installed headers. Normal objects outside ginpostinglist.o match B. Ordinary qualification compared valid decoder checksums and ordered Seq/index SQL/prepared/lifecycle references for B/D1/D2 and an assertion build. A separately reviewed 100-command smoke preceded the fixed 2,800-command screen (2,400 retained / 400 warm). An independent offline recount matched 78,000 numerical fields. There were no repeats, trimming, source retuning or control normalization. This finite serial warm-cache screen does not establish concurrency, disk-cold behavior, significance or a causal explanation for every whole-query difference.

RESULTS.md contains the complete absolute primary table and all remaining negatives; the accompanying JSON contains all three metrics, sample values, min/median/max/mean/sample stdev/MAD and direct pairs. Source, fixture, compiler flags, exact revisions, cleanup receipts and Linux reproduction commands are included. The small fresh build-and-qualification frontdoor has passed static/help checks only; its new Linux execution is not claimed. I have not selected D2 as a general upgrade on the basis of this screen.
