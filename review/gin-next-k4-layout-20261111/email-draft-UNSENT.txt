Subject: GIN scan-key layout control and complete builtin-memo comparison

Hi,

I tested one private GinScanKeyData field reorder with and without the
existing experimental builtin tsquery memo. Moving nadditional next to
nrequired gives a no-memo control C. Applying the same reorder to K3 gives
K4 with unchanged eligibility, arguments, ternary/recheck contract and
64-consecutive-miss policy. Installed-header assertions give B 152, C 144,
K3 160 and K4 152 bytes on the target LP64 ABI. This does not itself imply a
performance gain.

All five clean builds, core239 and posting-list checks, ordinary SQL,
prepared/write/delete/vacuum, ternary, budget and fallback qualification
passed. On Linux x86-64, release builds used GCC with -O2 -g -fno-lto
and LDFLAGS=-fno-lto; flags and paths were matched across variants.
The fixed comparison has 17 whole-SQL and seven actual-AM cases,
five roles including an identical-prefix/ELF twin, and two fresh cohorts
with balanced retained orders. Each complete attempt has 2880 raw / 2400
retained observations. Patches and a deterministic reproduction packet
include absolute times, spreads, every sample and all ten role pairs.

R1 is retained as a shared-load diagnostic after foreign CPU work was
observed at its tail with unknown overlap. Before reading accepted-run
timing values, we committed to the complete unchanged r2 repeat. Smoke
correctness outputs had already been reviewed. R2 used fresh data/socket
namespaces and a quieter monitored window; endpoint samples still cannot
prove absence of interference. Neither attempt is trimmed or pooled.

The repeat does not establish a general benefit from the reorder. K4/K3
whole-SQL executor differences are +0.178%/-0.134% on skew1000 and
+0.447%/-0.389% on separate_keys. Dense K4/B is +0.090%/-1.247%, with mixed
ranges. OR SQL K4/B is -4.744%/-5.122%, but K3/B is already -4.598%/-4.698%.
The absolute OR SQL medians are B 151.7905 -> K4 144.5640 ms and
B 151.6425 -> K4 144.9805 ms, each for the complete five-probe batch.
The percentages above are medians of direct paired ratios, not ratios
of those absolute medians. Actual-AM OR K4/K3 is -0.701%/-0.616%;
the much larger K4/B difference is
not the incremental effect of the new layout.

Controls also remain material: seq_control uses no GIN but K4/B changes
-2.428%/+1.250%; cohort-1 actual-AM OR/dense exact twins differ by -6.506%
and -6.175%. C alone makes absent AM +5.234%/+5.112% slower than B, while
K4/B there is +0.378%/+0.208%. We make no control-normalized estimate or
causal cache-line/allocator/code-placement attribution. All negative
outcomes and the original r1 diagnostic remain included.

Both attempts passed full numerical and semantic review: 114550 fields
per attempt, exact group completeness, matching plans/rows/loops/rechecks,
settings and answers. The independent peer extracts raw metrics without
importing the runtime validator. Arithmetic correctness does not resolve
identical-binary placement variation.

The cache idea already has direct context in Heikki's 2014 message [1].
Related extraction/build work is in David Geier's thread [2]. This packet
contributes a concrete layout-only control and reproducible observations,
not a new cache algorithm. It compares historical B72 plus one unchanged
fixedB prerequisite, with canonical alternative C and K3/K4 source paths;
it is not a current-master patch series or a broad speedup claim.
